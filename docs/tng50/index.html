How to read

  • Rows are lines, cool to hot. Columns are instrument cases (Layout).
  • Flux is normalised to the continuum; v is the velocity relative to the galaxy's systemic velocity.
  • Stepped line: the degraded spectrum, a Gaussian line-spread function of FWHM c/R with 5 pixels and the given S/N per resolution element. Shading: the noiseless model. Intrinsic (dashed): the noiseless spectrum before the line-spread function.
  • Fitted components: the fitted model in ink, each component in the line colour and a tick at its centre. A hollow tick marks a component on a bound, cut by the time cap, or unconstrained (log N error above 1 dex). An arrow marks a saturated component: its log N is only a lower limit, read as “log N > 14.5 sat”. The readout gives the fitted values, their errors and flags.
  • Drag or use the arrow keys to pan; ctrl or cmd with the wheel, or + and −, zoom; Home resets.
  • 3D: drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, click a sightline to open its spectra.

Data and methods

Sightlines through the circumgalactic medium of one TNG50-1 galaxy (subhalo 488530 at z = 0), labelled by orientation, impact parameter b and azimuth from the projected major axis.

Optical depths from Trident on a 0.25 km/s grid, stored at 1 km/s. Velocities are relative to the galaxy's systemic velocity.

H I: TNG neutral fraction (self-shielding included); metal ions: Trident HM2012 table, solar ratios scaled by metallicity.

Atomic data follow Trident 1.4.2 (O VI 1031.912 Å and C IV 1548.187 Å, 3 to 4 km/s from Morton 2003); O VII from Verner et al. 1996. Damped H I wings use Trident's Γ = 4.69 × 10⁸ s⁻¹ against the standard damping constant 6.265 × 10⁸ (Morton 2003), so damped log N fitted here sits about 0.13 dex above VPFIT-style fits of the same wings. Saturated components are lower limits.

The degradation and the Voigt fits run in your browser: Levenberg–Marquardt on (log N, b, v) through the line-spread function, seeded from the intrinsic component catalogue and pruned by ΔBIC. Noise matches the publication figure only statistically.

3D: every gas cell and star particle deposited into five nested 256³ cubes from ±400 kpc (L0) down to ±1.5 kpc (L4, 12 pc voxels) with 3D dust lanes, fetched from public Hugging Face datasets. 3D velocities are mass-weighted means per voxel in the galaxy's rest frame (no Hubble flow added; vx, vy and vz each clipped at the top of the 3D velocity window). vlos and σlos follow the camera ray every frame, and positive vlos recedes from you, as in the spectra; σlos is the spread between voxels, without thermal or within-voxel motion. Find searches the fitted components of every sightline. Data and credits

Simulation (507 galaxies across 9 simulation suites)

IllustrisTNG: TNG50-1 (198 Milky Way and M31 analogues), TNG100-1 (30 galaxies) and TNG300-1 (20 massive and group galaxies) at z = 0 (snapshot 99), from the TNG public data release (publicly available; access requires a free account and acceptance of the TNG terms of use). The simulations are described in Pillepich et al. 2018 (MNRAS 475, 648), Nelson et al. 2019a (Computational Astrophysics and Cosmology 6, 2), Nelson et al. 2019b (MNRAS 490, 3234) and Pillepich et al. 2019 (MNRAS 490, 3196), with the Milky Way and M31 sample of Pillepich et al. 2024 (MNRAS 535, 1721).

Illustris-1 cosmological hydrodynamic simulation at z = 0 (snapshot 135, 20 galaxies), from the Illustris public data release (Vogelsberger et al. 2014a, Nature 509, 177; Vogelsberger et al. 2014b, MNRAS 444, 1518; Nelson et al. 2015, Astronomy and Computing 13, 12).

FIRE-2 cosmological zoom-in simulations at z = 0 (snapshot 600, 23 halos), from the FIRE public data release (Wetzel et al. 2023, ApJS 265, 44; fire.northwestern.edu), run with GIZMO (Hopkins 2015, MNRAS 450, 53) and the FIRE-2 physics model (Hopkins et al. 2018, MNRAS 480, 800): Latte Milky Way-mass halos m12b, m12c, m12f, m12i, m12m, m12r, m12w and m12z (Wetzel et al. 2016, ApJL 827, L23; Garrison-Kimmel et al. 2017, MNRAS 471, 1709; Samuel et al. 2020, MNRAS 491, 1471), ELVIS on FIRE Local Group pairs Romeo and Juliet, Romulus and Remus, Thelma and Louise (Garrison-Kimmel et al. 2019, MNRAS 487, 1380), and Core dwarf halos m11b, m11d, m11e, m11h, m11i, m11q, m10q, m10v and m09 (Chan et al. 2018, MNRAS 478, 906; El-Badry et al. 2018, MNRAS 473, 1930).

SIMBA-25 & SIMBA-100 cosmological hydrodynamic simulations at z = 0 (snapshot 151, 100 galaxies across the m25n512 and m100n1024 volumes), from the SIMBA public data release (Davé et al. 2019, MNRAS 486, 2827), run with GIZMO meshless finite-mass hydrodynamics and two-mode AGN jet/radiative feedback.

Auriga Level-4 & Level-3 cosmological magnetohydrodynamic zoom-in simulations at z = 0 (67 MHD zoom-in halos across original Milky Way-mass, low-mass and dwarf series), from the Auriga public data release (Grand et al. 2017, MNRAS 467, 179; Grand et al. 2024, MNRAS 532, 1814), run with the moving-mesh magnetohydrodynamics code AREPO.

AGORA CosmoRun high-resolution galaxy simulation comparison project (1 Milky Way-mass zoom-in halo at z = 0, AREPO-T run), from the AGORA public data release (Kim et al. 2014, ApJS 210, 14; Kim et al. 2016, ApJ 833, 202; Roca-Fàbrega et al. 2021, ApJ 917, 64; Roca-Fàbrega et al. 2024, ApJ 968, 125).

CAMELS (Cosmology and Astrophysics with MachinE Learning Simulations) at z = 0 (48 galaxies: 4 matched 5-suite quintets across IllustrisTNG, SIMBA, Astrid, Swift-EAGLE and CROCODILE + 28 CAMELS-GZ28 zoom-in halos; camel-simulations.org; Villaescusa-Navarro et al. 2021, ApJ 915, 71; Villaescusa-Navarro et al. 2023, ApJS 265, 54).

We thank the IllustrisTNG, Illustris, FIRE, SIMBA, Auriga, AGORA and CAMELS collaborations and the Flatiron Institute for making these simulations public. Galaxy cutouts and snapshots from the public releases were processed into the spectra and 3D views shown here.

The IllustrisTNG, Illustris and Auriga simulations were undertaken with compute time awarded by the Gauss Centre for Supercomputing (GCS) under GCS Large-Scale Projects GCS-ILLU and GCS-DWAR on the GCS share of the supercomputer Hazel Hen at the High Performance Computing Center Stuttgart (HLRS), as well as on the machines of the Max Planck Computing and Data Facility (MPCDF) in Garching, Germany. The FIRE-2 simulations were run using XSEDE/ACCESS, NASA HEC, Blue Waters, Frontera and Flatiron Institute Scientific Computing Core resources. SIMBA was run on the DiRAC@Durham facility managed by the Institute for Computational Cosmology. CAMELS was run on the Flatiron Institute Scientific Computing Core cluster Rusty and XSEDE/ACCESS facilities.
