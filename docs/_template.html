How to read

  • Pick a line, then set the spectrograph (R, S/N) and the absorber (log N per component, Doppler b, separation Δv). Every panel follows.
  • S/N is per resolution element (one FWHM = c/R). Significances are expected (median) values; 3σ is the threshold throughout.
  • Spectrum: one component through the observing chain. Two components: the pair and the best single-component fit, with the residual it leaves against the noise per pixel.
  • Maps: click to move the spectrograph; the ring is the current setting. Table: click a row to switch line.

Model and methods

Every number is computed from atomic data (Trident line list, plus Morton 2003 and Verner et al. 1996 where Trident has no entry) and the same model as the published figures: Doppler cores (damping wings neglected), a Gaussian line-spread function of FWHM c/R, constant continuum noise, and matched-filter statistics.

Detection: Sdet = (S/N) K with K² = ∫D² dv / FWHM. Resolution: Δχ² of the best single-component fit (free N, b, v) to two equal components, Sres = (S/N)√G. Both are precomputed grids; the profiles and the single-component fit on this page are recomputed live in your browser.

Fiducial b combines the thermal width at a representative temperature with 5 km/s of turbulence. The explorer instead uses Voigt profiles with thermal b only. The noise illustration uses 3 pixels per resolution element.

Spectrograph markers: modes covering the line at z = 0, from a sourced table (COS, STIS, FUSE); the open circle is the typical COS-Halos S/N. Variants with the tabulated HST/COS line-spread function are in the repository.

From gas to spectrum

A cloud imprints an optical depth τ(v) on the background light. The spectrograph blurs the absorbed flux with its line-spread function (LSF) of width c/R, then samples it with noise. Play the sequence, or step through it.

The same chain on a simulated galaxy, from its gas cells to a fitted spectrum: How an absorption spectrum is made, including the blur and the noise.

Two components, one blurred trough

Two components separated by Δv blend when Δv is comparable to the effective width, FWHMeff = [(c/R)² + (1.665 b)²]1/2. The dip between them vanishes below the Sparrow separation ΔvS = 0.85 FWHMeff. A fit can still tell two from one if the noise is low enough.

The dashed curve is the best single-component impostor, refit live; the lower strip shows what it cannot reproduce, against the noise per pixel.

Requirement maps

Detect: the weakest single absorber detectable at 3σ, for the current b. Resolve: the smallest separation at which two equal components (current log N, fiducial b) are resolved at 3σ.

One law for every ion

For optically thin components the problem collapses to a single curve. Measure the separation in units of FWHMeff and the noise through Sdet, the significance with which the whole blend is detected. Equal pairs follow Δvmin ≈ 1.98 FWHMeff Sdet−1/4, so halving the separation costs 16× in S/N. The dot is the current setting.
